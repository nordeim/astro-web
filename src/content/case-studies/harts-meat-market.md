# Hart's Meat Market

A third-generation butcher shop that had quietly built one of the region's only whole-animal programs. Hart's came to us when they decided to open a tasting counter — a small standing-room-only bar inside the shop where customers could eat what the butchers cut. The brand needed to honor the family's three generations of butchery while making room for the new, more culinary identity the tasting counter implied.

## The Challenge

The Hart family had run the shop since 1974. The existing brand was a hand-painted sign from the 1980s — beautiful, but tied to a moment in time. The third generation, who'd taken over operations in 2019, wanted to expand the business without erasing the family's history. The tension: the tasting counter attracted a younger, foodie audience; the butcher shop's bread-and-butter was still the families and restaurants that had been buying from them for decades. The brand had to speak to both, in the same room, without feeling like two different businesses.

## Our Approach

We spent three days in the shop — one butchering alongside the team, two observing the tasting counter service. The strategic decision was to make the family name the brand's anchor and treat the tasting counter as a program *within* the shop, not a separate concept. The visual identity was rebuilt around the original 1974 sign's lettering (we had it digitized from a photograph), with a contemporary type system layered on top. The tasting counter got its own sub-mark — a small knife-and-fork glyph that signals "this is the eating part" without breaking from the parent brand.

## The Solution

- **Brand identity** — Restored 1974 wordmark as the primary mark, digitized from a photograph of the original shop sign; contemporary secondary type system for menus and signage; tasting-counter sub-mark for the in-shop eating program
- **Signage system** — Full exterior and interior signage refresh, including a hand-painted reproduction of the 1974 sign on the shopfront, wayfinding for the tasting counter, and a chalkboard-style cut-of-the-day system
- **Website** — Small static site focused on the butcher shop's hours, whole-animal program, and tasting-counter reservations; deliberately minimal — the shop is the experience, the site is the wayfinding

## The Result

The tasting counter reservations book out two weeks in advance. The signage refresh — particularly the restored 1974 shopfront sign — became a local-news story that drove a wave of new customers who'd driven past the shop for years without realizing it was there. The Hart family reports that long-time restaurant customers and new tasting-counter customers now interact in the same space without friction, which was the original brief.
