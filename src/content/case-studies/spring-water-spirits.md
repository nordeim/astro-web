# Spring Water Spirits

A sister brand to Marker 48 Brewing, Spring Water Spirits needed an identity that could stand on its own while reading as kin to the brewery's established coastal system. The brief was unusually specific: clarity and craft, with no nautical clichés.

## The Challenge

Spring Water Spirits distills in the same Weeki Wachee facility as Marker 48, but the two brands occupy different shelves and different occasions. The team didn't want a second coastal-beer aesthetic — they wanted a spirits-language vocabulary (bottle silhouette, tasting notes, batch cards) that signaled premium without going stiff. The website, meanwhile, had to do double duty as a retailer resource and a story-led brand experience for visitors who'd never heard of Weeki Wachee.

## Our Approach

We ran a one-day discovery at the distillery, tasting through the line and walking the source spring with the founders. The strategy that came out of it hinged on a single observation: every product in the line is cut with the same spring water, so the water — not the botanicals — should be the design hero. That decision cascaded into the type system (a high-contrast didone paired with a humanist sans), the photography direction (clear glass on wet stone), and the site architecture (a "source" page that sits between About and Shop).

## The Solution

- **Brand identity** — Refined wordmark with a custom "S" ligature that references the spring's surface ripple; palette anchored in cold-pressed ink and springwater aqua; bottle label system that scales from 50ml to 750ml
- **Website** — Story-driven site with a "source" page that anchors the brand geographically; retailer locator with inventory sync via the distillery's POS
- **Print collateral** — Tasting cards for each batch, with a perforated tear-off tasting-notes strip bartenders can hand to customers

## The Result

The brand launched in three Florida retailers and a single local bar program. Within the first quarter, distribution expanded to seven counties and the tasting-card system was adopted as a staff training tool by two of the retailers.
